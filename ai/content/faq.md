# FAQ

**H1:** Frequently Asked Questions About Call Handling for Spray Foam Contractors

## Cost & Value

**How much does an answering service charge?**
Pricing in the market generally follows one of three models: per-minute, per-call, or a flat monthly fee with an included call or minute allotment. Live-agent services tend to sit at the higher end of the market, AI voice-answering systems are often more predictable and moderate in cost, and missed-call text-back automation is typically the least expensive since it doesn't involve a live conversation. See our [full cost breakdown](/blog/how-much-does-answering-service-cost/) for more detail on how these models actually work.

**How much does an answering dispatch service cost per hour?**
Most answering services don't price by the hour — per-minute or per-call billing is far more common, sometimes with a flat monthly fee that includes a set number of minutes or calls before overage charges apply. "Per hour" isn't usually the right way to think about the cost structure for this category.

**Is an answering service worth it?**
It depends on your call volume, how often calls currently go unanswered, and your average job value — for a business missing even a few calls a week on jobs worth a few thousand dollars each, the math often favors some kind of paid solution. For a very low call-volume business, a free or low-cost option like missed-call text-back may be enough. See our [is it worth it](/blog/is-answering-service-worth-it/) breakdown for a way to think through your own numbers.

## Comparing Options

**What's the difference between an answering service and a virtual assistant?**
A traditional answering service focuses specifically on call handling — answering, taking messages, sometimes basic scheduling. A virtual assistant is typically a broader role that might include call handling alongside other administrative tasks (email, scheduling, basic bookkeeping). If call handling is your main concern, a dedicated answering service or AI voice system is usually a more targeted fit than a general virtual assistant.

**Is AI answering actually good enough, or do I need a real person?**
For straightforward calls — quote requests, scheduling questions, basic information — a well-configured AI system can handle the interaction competently and consistently. For more complex or emotionally charged calls (an upset customer, an unusual request), a live agent still generally offers more flexibility. See [Answering Service vs. AI vs. Voicemail](/answering-service-vs-ai-vs-voicemail/) for the fuller comparison.

**What if I already have voicemail — isn't that basically the same thing?**
Not really. Voicemail is passive and delayed — the caller leaves a message and waits for a callback, with no confirmation their call registered. An answering service, AI system, or even simple text-back automation responds immediately, which measurably changes whether a caller keeps looking for another contractor in the meantime.

## Does This Apply to My Business?

**Does this apply to a one-person crew, or only bigger companies?**
It applies to businesses of any size, and arguably matters more for smaller ones — a single-truck operation has less cushion to absorb a lost job than a larger company with more overall call volume and revenue.

**I only get a handful of calls a week — is a paid service overkill?**
Possibly. For low call volume, a lighter-weight option like missed-call text-back is often the more sensible starting point rather than a full answering service with a monthly minimum. See [Missed Call Text-Back](/missed-call-text-back/) for that option specifically.

**Does this only matter for residential jobs, or commercial too?**
Both — commercial and metal building inquiries are often higher-value and come with their own call-handling considerations. See [Spray Foam Call Handling](/spray-foam-call-handling/) for how commercial calls typically differ from residential ones.
